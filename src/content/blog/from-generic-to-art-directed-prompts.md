Ask an image model to "make it pretty" and you get the same gradients, glows and decorative flourishes as everyone else. I kept hitting that wall, until I watched a video and read a newsletter post that explained why, and what to do about it.

## The inspiration

Two Spanish-language resources shaped this project:

- **Video:** [Deja de generar imágenes genéricas con ChatGPT](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FX8c_ytLuIQ) ("Stop generating generic images with ChatGPT").
- **Post:** [Crear imágenes con ChatGPT: estilos, prompts y ejemplos](https://beailabs.beehiiv.com/p/crear-imagenes-chatgpt) by Beatriz Ruiz García, published in the beailabs newsletter.

The ideas below are my summary of them. For the details and the worked examples, go to the originals.

## What I took from them

**Decide before you generate.** The core argument is that vague instructions leave the model to fill the gaps with its defaults. If you make the design decisions first, you can judge the result against something and correct it.

**Use the same seven lines every time.** Format, what it is, theme, style, exact text, typography and palette, and what to avoid. Each line answers one question, so nothing important gets buried in a paragraph.

**Treat style as layers.** A design or layout language, an artistic movement and a technique or finish are different kinds of choices. Combining one of each is more precise than naming a single vibe.

**Put the exact text in quotes and list what to avoid.** Models are much better with a precise string than a description of one, and a short "avoid" list removes the clichés you already know are coming.

## What the Studio does with it

The Studio turns that checklist into a form. You describe the image, choose from curated formats, styles, fonts and palettes, and it compiles the seven lines into a prompt you can copy. Presets give you a starting point, and you can export and import a recipe so a good setup is reusable.

It is local-first: the recipe lives in your browser and nothing is sent anywhere.
